What Hellen lives with

Endometriosis, explained as simply as I can. Tap through slowly, no rush.

A gentle, simple explanation, not a medical guide. Her doctors know her body best.

First, the normal monthly thing

Every month the inside of the womb grows a soft, cozy lining, like fresh wallpaper, getting ready in case a baby comes. If none does, the wallpaper comes loose and leaves the body. That's a period. Totally normal.

womb

Now the wrong rooms

With endometriosis, tiny pieces of that same kind of lining grow in the wrong places, outside the womb. On the ovaries, the bowel, the wall of the belly. They were never supposed to be there.

Here's the cruel part

Those stray pieces still "hear" the monthly hormone signal, so they swell and bleed too, every single month. But outside the womb, the blood has no way out. It's like a tiny wound that reopens every month and can never get air to heal.

Tiny fires, and glue

Trapped blood irritates everything around it. The body lights tiny fires (inflammation) to deal with it, and that hurts. Over time it leaves scars that glue organs together, things that should slide freely get stuck. More pain, sometimes for days.

And it hides (the chameleon)

The signs look like so many other things, so women are often told "it's just a bad period." In Germany it takes 6 to 8 years on average to even get the right diagnosis. Years of real pain and not being believed. It is not just period pain.

The "Endobelly"

Many days her belly swells up like a balloon, sometimes she looks pregnant. It's so common it has a name: the Endobelly. Usually worst in the second half of her cycle. This is the condition, not something she ate or did wrong.

The tiny helpers in her belly

Her gut is full of tiny helpers (bacteria). When they fall out of balance, they can recycle the hormone that feeds the stray pieces, and make the gut leaky and inflamed. More fuel, more fire. That's why gentle, anti-inflammatory food really can help her feel better.

The part nobody sees

From the outside she can look completely fine. Smiling, working, normal. Inside it can be a storm. That gap, looking okay while hurting, is one of the loneliest parts. There's no cure yet, but it can be managed. It's not in her head. It's real.

So, how you actually help her

  • 💗Believe her, always. Never minimize. "It's just cramps" is the one thing to never say.
  • 🔥Warmth. A heat pad or Wärmflasche on a bad day is real medicine.
  • 🍲Gentle food on flare days. Small, warm, anti-inflammatory. Her meal helper is built for exactly this →
  • 🛋️Rest without guilt. The bloating and tiredness aren't laziness.
  • 🤍Be there on the hard days. Reliably. That's the one that matters most.

Now you understand the thing she carries quietly. That understanding, by itself, is a kind of medicine.
